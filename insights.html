Article

The UK international education market is changing, not closing

The UK's 2026 International Education Strategy puts student experience, quality outcomes and responsible recruitment at the centre, alongside an ambition to grow education exports. At the same time, regulation is tightening, with a proposed international student levy and a new approach to monitoring recruitment quality.

Sabbir Khan's view: the next five years will reward quality over volume, suitability over speed, compliance over shortcuts, and student outcomes over enrolment numbers. The better question isn't how to recruit more students, but how to recruit the right students and help them succeed.

Student advice

A university offer letter is not the finish line

Before accepting an offer, students should check seven things: whether the course fits their career, the full cost of study and living, the location, the support services available, graduate outcomes, whether their information is verified from official sources, and where they want to be in five years.

Research, compare, verify, then decide. A future deserves an informed decision, not just an offer letter.

Recruitment is only the beginning

Success in international education shouldn't be measured only by enrolment numbers. The real question is what happens to students after enrolment: do they gain skills, build careers and achieve better outcomes?

Recruitment, education, skills, employability, success: that is the full journey.

I would rather lose an enrolment than give a student the wrong advice

Responsible guidance sometimes means saying 'no', 'not this course' or 'not yet'. One enrolment has value today; trust lasts much longer.
