UK · October 2026

UK student visa bank balance rises from 30 November 2026

For Student visa applications made on or after 30 November 2026, the living-cost funds you must show rise to £1,570 per month in London and £1,203 per month outside London, for up to nine months (£14,130 and £10,827). Unpaid first-year tuition is on top of this.

What it means for you: if you're applying for a January 2027 intake, check which figure applies on your application date, keep the money in your account for the full 28 days, and don't cut it close. Full details in my Study in the UK guide.

Source: Study Abroad Edu

UK · October 2026

UK universities now face much tougher visa compliance rules

Since 1 June 2026, UK universities must keep student visa refusal rates below 5% (previously 10%) and enrolment rates at or above 95% (previously 90%). Course completion must be at least 85%, rising to 90% from June 2027. A new red-amber-green rating is based on each university's weakest score, and ratings will be made public from 2027.

What it means for you: universities will check applicants much more carefully before issuing a CAS. Expect closer questions about your funds, English, academic progression and any past visa refusals. Genuine, well-prepared students will be fine; weak or inconsistent applications will struggle.

Source: ICEF Monitor · Garth Coates

UK · October 2026

UK Graduate Route cut to 18 months from 1 January 2027

The UK's post-study work visa, the Graduate Route, is currently 2 years for bachelor's and master's graduates. For most applications made from 1 January 2027, it falls to 18 months. PhD graduates keep 3 years.

What it means for you: if you finish your course in late 2026, check whether you can apply before 1 January 2027. If you're starting now, plan your job search from your first term, not your last.

Source: ICEF Monitor

UK · October 2026

England confirms £925 International Student Levy from August 2028

The government plans to charge universities in England £925 per international student per year from 1 August 2028, after trial calculations using 2026/27 and 2027/28 data. Each university's first 220 international students are exempt. The levy is charged to universities, not directly to students.

What it means for you: you won't pay this levy yourself, but some universities may adjust fees, scholarships or recruitment plans. Compare offers carefully for 2028 entry.

Source: ICEF Monitor · GOV.UK

USA · October 2026

New international students in the USA fall 20%

A NAFSA survey of 149 US institutions found new international enrolment fell 20% for spring 2026, with an average 24% drop at graduate level. Most institutions blamed restrictive government policies. In September 2026, a federal judge also blocked a proposed rule that would have replaced the flexible "duration of status" system with fixed admission periods; the existing system stays in place for now, though the government may appeal.

What it means for you: the USA still offers world-class universities and strong STEM OPT options, and lower competition can mean better scholarship chances. But build extra time into your visa plan and follow official updates closely. See my Study in the USA guide.

Source: TIME · Mwakilishi

Canada · October 2026

Canada is bouncing back, especially for master's students

Searches for studying in Canada jumped 55% year on year in December 2025, according to Keystone Education Group, and IDP's 2026 survey shows Canada gaining ground as a preferred destination. The main driver: master's and doctoral students at public institutions are now exempt from the study permit cap and the PAL requirement. Study permit approval rates have also improved.

What it means for you: Canada is once again a strong choice for postgraduate study. Remember that proof of funds is now CAD 23,448 for a single applicant (from 1 September 2026), and check PGWP eligibility before you apply. See my Study in Canada guide.

Source: ICEF Monitor · ICA Immigration

Australia · October 2026

Australia holds 2027 student planning level at 295,000

Australia's National Planning Level for new international students stays at 295,000 for 2027, and no active provider will receive a lower allocation than in 2026. Visa applications are prioritised until a provider reaches 80% of its allocation, then processed more slowly. Separately, new rules announced in September 2026 mean most new students can no longer bring partners or children, with exemptions including PhD candidates.

What it means for you: apply early in the cycle, while your chosen university still has priority processing places, and plan for studying without family unless you qualify for an exemption.

Source: Study Australia · Mwakilishi

Global · October 2026

Asia rising: Seoul and Tokyo top the world's best student cities

The QS Best Student Cities 2027 ranking puts Seoul first, Tokyo second and London third, and 16 UK cities make the list, more than any other country. Singapore, Beijing and Kuala Lumpur all climbed, while several North American cities fell, mainly because of affordability.

What it means for you: London and the UK remain among the world's top student destinations, but it's worth looking beyond the "big four". Malaysia, South Korea, Japan and Singapore offer quality degrees at lower cost.

Source: ICEF Monitor

Global · October 2026

OECD: many international graduates don't stay to work

An OECD analysis of six major destinations found that five years after studying, about 52% of international students remained in Canada and Germany, around a third in Australia and France, 19% in the Netherlands and just 7% in the UK, even though more than 70% of students in some countries planned to stay. The report warns that recruitment messages can oversell job and residence prospects.

What it means for you: don't choose a country only for post-study work. Choose a course with clear career value at home and abroad, use careers services from day one, and treat post-study work as a bonus, not a guarantee. This is exactly why I believe in honest advice over easy enrolments.

Source: ICEF Monitor

Sabbir Khan's takeaway

2026 is the year international education became more selective everywhere. The UK, Australia and the USA are all tightening, while Canada is reopening for postgraduates and Asia is rising fast. For students, the winners will be those who plan early, choose courses for real career outcomes, keep finances and documents clean, and work only with honest, university-approved advisers.

Frequently asked questions

What are the biggest study abroad changes in 2026?

In the UK: higher bank balance requirements from 30 November 2026, stricter university compliance rules since June 2026, and the Graduate Route falling to 18 months from January 2027. Canada exempted master's and PhD students at public institutions from the study permit cap and PAL. Australia kept its 2027 planning level at 295,000 and restricted family members for most new students. New international enrolments in the USA fell 20% in spring 2026.

Is it still a good time to study in the UK?

Yes, for well-prepared students. The UK still offers short, high-quality degrees, and London ranks third in the QS Best Student Cities 2027. But visa funds are rising and universities are screening applicants more carefully, so prepare your finances and documents early.

Which country is easiest for international students in 2026?

No country is "easy", but Canada has become more welcoming for master's and PhD students after removing them from the study permit cap and PAL requirement, and Asian destinations such as Malaysia and South Korea are growing fast.

Will the UK international student levy increase my fees?

The £925 levy, planned from August 2028, is charged to universities in England, not to students directly. Some universities may adjust fees or scholarships in response, so compare offers carefully for 2028 entry.
