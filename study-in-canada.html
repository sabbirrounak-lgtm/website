Why study in Canada?

Canada offers high-quality public universities and colleges, a welcoming multicultural society and, for eligible graduates, the chance to gain Canadian work experience after studying. Programmes range from 1-year college certificates to 4-year bachelor's degrees and research master's and PhDs.

Important: Canada's study permit rules have changed

Since 2024, Canada has capped the number of new study permit applications. For the 2026 calendar year, the cap allows 309,670 study permit application spaces for students who need an attestation letter. Most applicants need a Provincial or Territorial Attestation Letter (PAL/TAL) from the province where they will study, which the institution helps arrange.

From 1 January 2026, master's and doctoral students at public institutions are exempt from needing a PAL/TAL. Undergraduate and college students usually still need one. Confirm on canada.ca.

Intakes and how to apply

  • September (Fall): the main intake.
  • January (Winter): widely offered, especially by colleges.
  • May (Summer): limited programmes.

Apply only to a Designated Learning Institution (DLI). After you receive your Letter of Acceptance and PAL/TAL (if needed), apply for your study permit online.

The living-cost amount is updated each year and is higher if family members come with you. Many students use a Guaranteed Investment Certificate (GIC) from a participating Canadian bank as part of their proof of funds.

Working while you study and after graduation

  • During your studies: eligible students can work off campus for a limited number of hours per week during academic sessions and full time during scheduled breaks. Check the current weekly limit on canada.ca.
  • Post-Graduation Work Permit (PGWP): eligible graduates can get an open work permit of up to 3 years depending on programme length. There are now language-test requirements, and for many college graduates the programme must be in an eligible field of study. Check eligibility before choosing your programme.

Choosing the right programme in Canada

  • Check that the institution is a DLI and that the programme is PGWP-eligible if you plan to work after graduating.
  • Compare universities and colleges: colleges often offer shorter, career-focused diplomas, while universities offer degrees and research programmes.
  • Think about province: cost of living, job market and attestation availability differ.
  • Look at co-op programmes, which build paid work experience into your studies.

Frequently asked questions

How much money do I need for a Canada study permit?

For applications from 1 September 2026, a single applicant outside Quebec must show CAD 23,448 for living costs, plus first-year tuition and travel. Quebec has its own requirements. Check canada.ca for the latest figure.

Do I need a PAL to study in Canada?

Most undergraduate and college students need a Provincial or Territorial Attestation Letter. From 1 January 2026, master's and doctoral students at public institutions are exempt. Your institution will guide you.

Is there a cap on Canadian study permits in 2026?

Yes. For 2026, Canada set 309,670 study permit application spaces for applicants who need an attestation letter.

Can I work in Canada after I graduate?

If you are eligible, you can apply for a Post-Graduation Work Permit of up to 3 years. Eligibility depends on your programme, institution, field of study and language test results.

Can I work while studying in Canada?

Yes, eligible students can work off campus for a limited number of hours per week during term and full time during scheduled breaks.
