Why study abroad?

Studying abroad gives international students a globally recognised qualification, English-language fluency, international work experience and a professional network that lasts a career. For students from Bangladesh, India, Pakistan, Nepal, Sri Lanka, Nigeria and the Middle East, the most popular destinations remain the UK, Canada and the USA, followed by Australia and Europe.

But the best destination is not the most popular one. It's the one that fits your course, budget, career plan and long-term goals. After more than 12 years helping universities recruit international students, the most expensive mistake I see is choosing a country, or a university, for the wrong reasons.

How to apply to study abroad: step by step

  1. Start 12–18 months early. The main intakes are September/Fall (all three countries) and January/Winter (UK and Canada). Some UK universities also offer May intakes.
  2. Choose the course first, then the country. Ask: what job will this degree realistically lead to?
  3. Shortlist 5–8 universities using official rankings, graduate outcomes, location and total cost, not just tuition.
  4. Prepare documents: passport, academic transcripts and certificates, English test (IELTS, PTE, TOEFL or Duolingo where accepted), personal statement / statement of purpose, references, CV.
  5. Apply directly, through UCAS (UK undergraduate), or through a university-approved education agent.
  6. Accept an offer and pay the deposit to receive your CAS (UK), Letter of Acceptance (Canada) or I-20 (USA).
  7. Arrange funds: tuition plus the living-cost amount each visa requires, held for the required period.
  8. Apply for your visa on the official government website and book biometrics or an interview.
  9. Plan arrival: accommodation, travel, health cover, and pre-departure briefings.

7 questions to ask before you accept an offer

  1. Is this course right for my career?
  2. Can I afford the full cost: tuition, accommodation, food, transport, visa and emergencies?
  3. Is the location right for me, including living costs and industry access?
  4. What academic, careers and wellbeing support will I get?
  5. What are graduates of this course doing now?
  6. Have I verified the information from official university and government sources, not just social media?
  7. Where do I want to be five years from now, and does this choice get me there?

The cheapest option isn't always the best value, and an offer letter isn't the finish line. Research, compare, verify, then decide.

Choosing a study abroad consultant or agent

A good education agent can save you time and mistakes. A bad one can cost you your visa. Look for an agent who is officially appointed by the universities they recommend (ask to see it on the university's website), who is trained (for example, British Council certified counsellors for the UK), and who is willing to tell you "no", "not this course" or "not yet". Avoid anyone who promises a guaranteed visa, asks you to submit false documents, or pushes one university without explaining why.

Scholarships and funding

  • University scholarships: most UK, Canadian and US universities offer merit scholarships or tuition discounts for international students. Check each university's international scholarships page.
  • Government scholarships: for example Chevening and Commonwealth Scholarships (UK), and the Fulbright Program (USA).
  • Assistantships: in the USA especially, research and teaching assistantships can cover tuition for master's and PhD students.

Frequently asked questions

Which country is best for international students to study abroad?

There is no single best country. The UK suits students who want a shorter degree (a 1-year master's); Canada suits those focused on long-term career pathways; the USA suits research, STEM and students who want flexible majors and scholarship options. Choose the course and career outcome first, then the country.

How much does it cost to study abroad?

Costs vary widely by country, city and university. As well as tuition, budget for living costs, visa and health fees, travel and an emergency fund. Each visa also requires proof that you hold a set amount for living costs; see the UK, Canada and USA guides on this site for current figures.

When should I start applying to study abroad?

Ideally 12–18 months before your intended start date. Popular courses fill early, and visa processing, funds and English tests all take time.

Can I study abroad without IELTS?

Sometimes. Many universities accept PTE, TOEFL or Duolingo, and some accept medium-of-instruction letters or school English results. However, visa rules and university requirements differ, so check each university's English language page.

Can I work while studying abroad?

In most cases yes, with limits. The UK usually allows up to 20 hours per week in term for degree-level students; Canada allows limited off-campus work; the USA mainly allows on-campus work in your first year. Always check your own visa conditions.
